## Image 01 Deep Analysis

Source dimensions: 1280x1708, approximately 3:4.

### First Impression

Image 01 reads as a floating translucent tote/basket containing mango-like fruit forms. It is not a product render, not a clean vector icon, not a literal painting, and not a source photo with a blur or frosted-glass filter. Its appeal comes from the tension between:

- a very large, pale, almost disappearing frosted container;
- saturated fruit colors glowing from inside;
- soft haze and matte grain that make the whole object feel quiet and touchable;
- a centered still-life poster layout with lots of white air.

The "main object" is not the fruit alone. It is the relationship between the frosted outer bag and the fruit color seen through it.

From far away, image 01 reads almost like three simple value/color zones: white field, pale translucent shell, and warm fruit glow. From close up, the attraction comes from the slight material noise, the soft crossing layers, and the way edges appear and disappear. This is the balance to preserve.

The image feels redrawn: the bag, handles, fruit, and lip are simplified into invented soft shapes. It does not preserve a camera snapshot, exact fabric folds, realistic lighting, or literal object texture. The reference logic is "reconstruct the subject as a new soft poster object," not "put frosted glass over an existing photo."

### Composition

- The subject is very large and horizontally centered.
- The translucent tote occupies almost the full visual width, but because it is low contrast it does not feel crowded.
- The saturated fruit mass sits lower-middle, roughly around the visual center.
- The handle arcs into the upper third and is extremely faint, functioning as a ghost contour.
- There is little conventional top text. The bottom has a small handwritten caption, far away from the subject.
- The layout is airy because the outer shell is pale and semi-transparent, not because the subject is tiny.

Skill implication:

- Do not shrink every subject too much.
- Large pale translucent forms can occupy a lot of space without feeling heavy.
- Keep strong color mass inside the subject, not scattered across the page.
- Keep the poster rules fixed: the output remains 3:4, pure white, centered, with top name and bottom caption even when the reference itself has a different text layout.
- Do not preserve the source photo's camera angle, background, realistic lighting, or detail density. Compose a new poster object from the extracted cues.

### Shape Language

- The tote/basket is reduced to a few big soft forms: bowl-like base, back wall, front lip, handles.
- Edges are irregular and organic, not geometric. They look like soft fabric/plastic/acrylic, not hard vector polygons.
- The fruit is simplified into two rounded glowing masses, not detailed mango renderings.

- The handles are narrow, pale, and semi-transparent; they add identity without drawing attention.
- The silhouette feels slightly graphic because it is simplified, but its borders are not vector-hard. They feather, blur, and fade into the white field.

Skill implication:

- Preserve the object's key structural cues, especially the parts that cross in front of or behind other forms.
- Use organic broad silhouettes.
- Avoid hard polygon-only abstraction.
- Do not interpret "flat" as solid vector. Interpret it as broad simplified translucent planes with very weak depth.
- Redesign these silhouettes. Do not trace the source contours exactly and blur them afterward.

### Transparency

Transparency in image 01 is not "clear glass." It is milky, hazy, and partial.

- The bag wall is visible mostly by its pale pink/gray tint and softly brighter edges.
- The fruit color bleeds through the bag wall, especially along the front lip and lower container.
- Some far edges almost vanish into the white background.
- The front lip covers the fruit but still lets orange/red color glow through.
- The overlapping handle on the right is visible as a pale vertical band over warm color.

Skill implication:

- Ask for milky translucency, not crystal transparency.
- Color should be strongest behind a frosted layer.
- Overlapping parts are essential. Transparency must be visible through layering.
- Include at least one crossing pale layer, such as a lip, rim, flap, handle, wrapper edge, pot wall, or shell edge, over the strongest color field.
- The crossing layer must be part of the redrawn object, not a generic full-image glass pane.

## Cross-Reference Principles

Use these principles for every generated image:

1. **Semantic extraction first:** Extract identity, silhouette, color masses, and 3-5 cues from the photo before generating.
2. **Redraw, do not filter:** Create a new poster illustration. Do not blur the source photo, trace it, or place a frosted glass layer on top.
3. **Outer translucent carrier:** Identify what part of the object can become the frosted carrier: cup wall, bag wall, box liner, pot wall, shell, wrapper, fabric, casing. If no natural carrier exists, redraw the object's own surfaces as milky color planes instead of adding a generic membrane.
4. **Inner color field:** Put the object's strongest colors inside or behind that carrier.
5. **Layer proof:** Include at least one foreground translucent edge/lip/handle/flap crossing over a color field.
6. **Soft realism, not flat icon:** Use soft gradients and broad highlights, but no hard product-render shadows.
7. **Large simplification:** Keep the big form and iconic cues; remove tiny realism.
8. **Locked poster:** 3:4 format, pure white background, centered subject, top bilingual object name, bottom creator/mood caption.
